## Limitations — read this before using the table

**1. The two authoritative sources could not be read.**
The CUNY GC Economics program publishes exactly what this task asks for, in two places:

- [Alumni Highlights](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/economics/alumni-highlights) — job placements
  organized by graduating year, with expandable sections for 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026.
- The per-year job market candidate pages
  ([2024–25](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/economics/2024-2025-job-market-candidates),
  [2025–26](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/economics/2025-2026-job-market-candidates)), plus the
  [Fall 2022–Fall 2024 program newsletter (PDF)](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Econ-program-Newsletter-Dec-2024.pdf)
  and the [2022–23 candidates packet (PDF)](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/Job-Market-Candidates-Seeking-Employment_2022-2023.pdf).

Neither could be retrieved: outbound HTTPS from the environment used to build this file is
restricted by an egress policy, and every direct page fetch — `gc.cuny.edu` included —
returned HTTP 403 from the proxy. The table was therefore assembled from search-engine
indexes only. **Anyone with ordinary browser access should open the Alumni Highlights page
first**; it will likely fill in most of the empty cells here in a few minutes and supersede
the "Data not publicly available" entries.

**2. "Initial placement" vs. "current position."**
The task asks for the job accepted *immediately after graduation*. For several people the
only publicly documented fact is where they work *now*, which may be a second or third job.
Those cells say so explicitly instead of presenting a current employer as an initial
placement. Do not read "currently at X" as "placed at X."

**3. Job market papers are the weakest column.**
JMP titles are rarely archived — candidates replace the paper on their website with the
published version and the "Job Market Paper" label disappears. Only three JMP titles here
are documented as such (Desai, Asher, Landge), each from the candidate's own CV or a JMP-
labeled PDF. For everyone else the dissertation title is given where known, and the JMP cell
says the title is not publicly available. A dissertation title is **not** a JMP title.

**4. Graduation years marked "c."** are inferred from the position of the dissertation record
in the CUNY Academic Works sequence relative to dated neighbors, not from a stated date.

**5. Known graduates missing from the table.** Coverage is thinnest for 2021 and 2023 —
neither year's dissertation cohort surfaced fully in search indexes.

## How to extend this file

1. Open [Alumni Highlights](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/economics/alumni-highlights) and expand
   each year 2021–2026. That is the program's own placement record.
2. Cross-check names against the
   [Economics dissertations collection](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/gc_econ_diss/) for
   exact graduation dates and dissertation titles.
3. For each name, look for a personal website or CV PDF — that is where a JMP title, if it
   survives anywhere, will be found. The GC's [Stone Center](https://stonecenter.gc.cuny.edu/about/graduate-students/)
   hosts CV PDFs for its Junior Scholars.
